When to send wedding invitations and save the dates in Ireland

By Evan, Paper Tide · 16 November 2026

Timing your stationery well takes a lot of stress out of wedding planning. Send too late and guests may already have plans; too early and invitations get lost on the fridge. Here’s a simple, practical timeline for Irish weddings, whether you’re marrying at home or abroad.

The quick answer

  • Save the dates: 9 to 12 months before the wedding.
  • Invitations: 8 to 12 weeks before.
  • RSVP deadline: 4 to 6 weeks before.
  • Thank-you cards: within about three months after the day.

These are guidelines rather than rules. Adjust them for your venue, your guests and the time of year.

When to send save the dates

Save the dates give guests an early heads-up so they can book time off, arrange childminding and plan travel. For most weddings in Ireland, 9 to 12 months before the day works well.

Send them earlier, up to 12 to 18 months ahead, if:

  • You’re getting married abroad, or many guests are travelling from overseas.
  • Your date falls on a bank holiday weekend, at Christmas or in the height of summer, when diaries and hotels fill fast.
  • Your venue is remote and guests will need to book accommodation early.

Only send a save the date to people you’re certain to invite. Once it’s in the post, an invitation needs to follow.

When to send wedding invitations

For a wedding in Ireland, aim to post invitations 8 to 12 weeks before the day. That gives guests enough notice without the date sneaking up on them, and it gives you time to chase replies.

For a wedding abroad, send them a little earlier, around 3 to 4 months before, so guests can confirm flights and accommodation. If you sent save the dates, the invitation’s job is to confirm the details and collect RSVPs.

Setting your RSVP date

Ask for replies 4 to 6 weeks before the wedding. Check with your venue first: most will want final numbers a few weeks out, and you’ll need time to finish your table plan, menus and place names before those go to print. Working backwards from the venue’s deadline is the easiest way to set the date.

A working timeline for your stationery

  1. 12 to 14 months before: choose your collection and confirm your date and venue.
  2. 10 to 12 months before: order and post your save the dates.
  3. 4 to 5 months before: finalise your guest list and invitation wording.
  4. 3 to 4 months before: order your invitations, allowing time for proofs and printing.
  5. 8 to 12 weeks before: post your invitations.
  6. 4 to 6 weeks before: RSVPs close; chase anyone who hasn’t replied.
  7. 3 to 4 weeks before: order menus and place names once numbers and the table plan are final.
  8. Within 3 months after: send your thank-you cards.

Don’t forget the legal paperwork

If you’re marrying in Ireland, you must give notice of your intention to marry to the civil registration service at least three months before your wedding, whatever type of ceremony you’re having. It’s worth sorting this around the same time as your save the dates, so it doesn’t get forgotten. Check the official guidance on gov.ie for current requirements.

Leave time for proofs and printing

The step couples most often underestimate is the time between choosing a design and having printed cards in hand. Leave a few weeks for wording, proofs and any changes, then printing and delivery. With semi-custom stationery that’s much quicker than a fully bespoke design, but a little breathing room keeps it enjoyable. As a rule of thumb, get in touch with us about a month before you want your stationery in the post.

Digital and printed together

Lots of couples now combine printed invitations with a digital version. It’s handy for guests abroad, for chasing late replies, and for collecting dietary requirements through an RSVP page. Our digital invitation with RSVP page matches your printed collection, so everything still feels like one set.
